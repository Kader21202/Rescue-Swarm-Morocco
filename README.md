\# Rescue Swarm Morocco



\## Intelligent Maritime Rescue System Using AI-Controlled Adaptive Hydrodynamic Action



\*\*Status: Independent research and development project — ongoing.\*\*



Rescue Swarm Morocco is an intelligent robotic maritime rescue project designed to detect, reach, secure, safeguard, and evacuate multiple people in distress at sea, including passive or non-cooperative victims.



\## Project Objective



The project explores a coordinated maritime rescue architecture in which autonomous robotic systems and artificial intelligence work together to reduce rescue delays and human rescuer exposure in complex multi-victim emergencies.



The target architecture combines:



\- an unmanned \*\*Rescue Carrier\*\* for rapid deployment and evacuation;

\- \*\*ScoutPods\*\* for search, observation, and victim localization;

\- \*\*RescuePods\*\* for controlled approach and individual victim recovery;

\- temporary \*\*Rescue Islands\*\* for safeguarding victims while evacuation operations continue;

\- an AI-supported mission architecture coordinating perception, planning, task allocation, navigation, rescue operations, and system reconfiguration.



\## Role of Artificial Intelligence



Artificial intelligence is considered at the global system level rather than as an isolated component.



Its intended roles include:



\- perception and victim localization;

\- interpretation of the evolving rescue situation;

\- mission planning and prioritization;

\- allocation of tasks between robotic units;

\- assistance with navigation and controlled victim approach;

\- coordination of multiple simultaneous rescue operations;

\- management of evacuation sequences;

\- dynamic reconfiguration when conditions or available resources change.



Safety-critical actuator commands are intended to remain subject to independent deterministic safety constraints.



\## Research Program



The project also includes experimental research on:



\- robotic acquisition of passive victims;

\- airway-aware acquisition strategies;

\- controlled interaction between robotic rescue systems and victims;

\- adaptive hydrodynamic action as a potential assistance mechanism;

\- coordination of multiple autonomous rescue units.



Experimental capabilities are treated as research hypotheses until validated.



\## Current Stage



The project is currently in the \*\*research, system-design, and experimental-preparation stage\*\*.



Current work includes system architecture, RescuePod design, AI mission architecture, experimental protocols, and planning for progressive physical validation.



No unvalidated experimental capability is presented as an established result.
