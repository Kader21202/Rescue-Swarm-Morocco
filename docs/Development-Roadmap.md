\# Rescue Swarm Morocco — Development Roadmap



\## Development Principle



Rescue Swarm Morocco is intended to be developed progressively.



The complete system will not be built as a single step. Each subsystem must first be designed, prototyped, instrumented and experimentally evaluated before integration into the broader rescue architecture.



\## Phase 1 — RescuePod Prototype



The immediate priority is the construction and validation of a robust RescuePod.



Initial objectives include:



\- remote-controlled navigation;

\- protected propulsion;

\- low-speed controlled approach;

\- soft interaction with a rescue mannequin;

\- experimental instrumentation;

\- measurement of acquisition performance;

\- documented and filmed testing.



Initial trials are intended to begin under controlled and calm-water conditions.



\## Phase 2 — Instrumented RescuePod



The RescuePod will progressively integrate additional sensing and measurement capabilities.



Potential instrumentation includes:



\- cameras;

\- positioning sensors;

\- inertial sensing;

\- contact and force measurement;

\- propulsion monitoring;

\- experimental victim-state observation.



The purpose of this phase is to establish a measurable physical platform before introducing higher levels of autonomy.



\## Phase 3 — Assisted Approach and Recovery



Once the physical RescuePod has been sufficiently characterized, assisted control functions may be introduced.



Research will focus on:



\- controlled approach trajectories;

\- speed reduction near victims;

\- obstacle and victim-relative positioning;

\- safe recovery assistance;

\- deterministic safety constraints.



\## Phase 4 — Supervised Autonomy



The next stage will investigate supervised autonomous behavior.



Potential functions include:



\- victim localization;

\- approach planning;

\- dynamic trajectory adjustment;

\- mission-state estimation;

\- operator supervision and emergency override.



\## Phase 5 — Rescue Carrier



A larger unmanned Rescue Carrier will then be developed to transport multiple rescue modules and evacuate rescued victims.



Development areas include:



\- hull and stability;

\- propulsion;

\- energy management;

\- payload capacity;

\- module deployment;

\- victim-position allocation;

\- navigation and communication;

\- return-to-shore operations.



\## Phase 6 — Multi-Pod Operations



Several ScoutPods and RescuePods will be progressively integrated into coordinated experiments.



Research objectives include:



\- distributed search;

\- victim assignment;

\- simultaneous rescue operations;

\- communication management;

\- mission reconfiguration following unit failure.



\## Phase 7 — Rescue Islands



Temporary floating Rescue Islands will be integrated to support multi-victim operations.



Their role will be evaluated for:



\- temporary victim safeguarding;

\- position maintenance;

\- communication and tracking;

\- transfer between RescuePods and Rescue Carrier;

\- autonomous return when empty.



\## Phase 8 — Integrated Rescue Swarm



The final target is an integrated rescue architecture combining:



\- Rescue Station;

\- Rescue Carrier;

\- ScoutPods;

\- RescuePods;

\- Rescue Islands;

\- mission-level artificial intelligence;

\- independent deterministic safety mechanisms;

\- human supervision.



The integrated system will require progressive validation under increasingly realistic maritime conditions.



\## Research Integration



Experimental research, including SAR-A and adaptive hydrodynamic action, will only be incorporated into operational prototypes if supported by experimental evidence.



Failure or non-identifiable results remain valid outcomes and may lead to modification or termination of individual research branches.



\## Current Priority



\*\*Current priority: Phase 1 — RescuePod design and experimental preparation.\*\*



Project status: \*\*ongoing research and development — 2026.\*\*
