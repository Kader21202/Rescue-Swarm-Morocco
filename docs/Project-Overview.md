\# Rescue Swarm Morocco — Project Overview



\## Project Definition



Rescue Swarm Morocco is an independent research and development project exploring an intelligent, modular and coordinated robotic system for maritime rescue operations involving one or multiple victims.



The project is designed around a fundamental operational principle:



\*\*High power and speed away from victims; slow, controlled and safety-constrained interaction near victims.\*\*



Its long-term objective is to develop a system capable of detecting, reaching, securing, temporarily safeguarding and evacuating people in distress at sea, including passive or non-cooperative victims.



\## Target System Architecture



The proposed architecture is composed of several complementary robotic elements:



\### Rescue Carrier



An unmanned maritime vehicle designed to rapidly transport rescue modules to an accident area and subsequently evacuate rescued victims to shore.



The Rescue Carrier is intended to operate without an onboard pilot or rescue crew.



\### ScoutPods



Small autonomous or supervised robotic units intended for search, observation, localization and situational assessment.



\### RescuePods



Compact robotic rescue units intended to approach individual victims at controlled speed and assist in their recovery and stabilization.



\### Rescue Islands



Mobile floating platforms intended to provide temporary safe holding positions for rescued victims while the Rescue Carrier performs evacuation rotations.



Rescue Islands are not intended to replace the Rescue Carrier for evacuation to shore.



\## Role of Artificial Intelligence



Artificial intelligence is considered as a global coordination and decision-support layer across the rescue mission.



Its intended functions include:



\- perception and victim localization;

\- interpretation of the evolving rescue environment;

\- mission planning;

\- prioritization and task allocation;

\- navigation assistance;

\- coordination of ScoutPods and RescuePods;

\- management of temporary safeguarding and evacuation;

\- adaptation to changing environmental conditions;

\- system reconfiguration following equipment failure or mission changes.



Safety-critical actions are intended to remain constrained by independent deterministic safety mechanisms.



\## Research Components



The project includes experimental research concerning the interaction between robotic rescue systems, water dynamics and passive victims.



Current research directions include:



\- robotic acquisition of passive victims;

\- airway-aware acquisition strategies;

\- controlled soft interaction during victim recovery;

\- experimental investigation of adaptive hydrodynamic action;

\- multi-agent rescue coordination.



These research directions are treated as experimental questions.



No unvalidated capability is presented as an established result.



\## Development Strategy



Development is planned progressively:



1\. RescuePod remote-controlled prototype;

2\. instrumented RescuePod;

3\. assisted approach and recovery;

4\. supervised autonomy;

5\. Rescue Carrier development;

6\. deployment of multiple coordinated Pods;

7\. integration of temporary Rescue Islands;

8\. progressive validation of the complete Rescue Swarm architecture.



Each stage is intended to be experimentally evaluated before integration into subsequent stages.



\## Current Status



\*\*Research and development ongoing — Morocco, 2026.\*\*



The project is currently focused on system architecture, experimental preparation and the definition of the first RescuePod validation phase.
