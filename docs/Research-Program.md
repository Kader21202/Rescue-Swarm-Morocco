\# Rescue Swarm Morocco — Research Program



\## 1. Research Objective



The Rescue Swarm Morocco research program investigates technical and scientific questions arising from robotic maritime rescue, particularly when a victim is passive, unconscious, non-cooperative, or difficult to recover safely.



The research program is separated from the operational target architecture.



A capability is not considered validated merely because it appears technically plausible or is included in the future system architecture.



Experimental questions must be tested independently before operational integration.



\## 2. Research Method



The project follows a progressive experimental approach:



\*\*Research question → prior-art investigation → candidate mechanism → falsification-oriented experiment → measurement → analysis → decision\*\*



Negative, null and non-identifiable results are retained as valid experimental outcomes.



A research direction may be abandoned when experimental evidence does not support it.



\## 3. SAR-A



\### Airway-Aware Robotic Aquatic Acquisition



SAR-A investigates whether explicitly considering the relationship between a passive victim's mouth/nose and the water surface during robotic acquisition can produce measurably different recovery strategies.



The central experimental question is:



\*\*Can real-time information about mouth/nose exposure to the water be used as feedback for the next physical acquisition action while maintaining mechanical safety constraints?\*\*



The research does not assume that such a strategy will outperform conventional acquisition.



It must be experimentally tested.



\### Initial Experimental Variables



Candidate measurements include:



\- capture success;

\- capture duration;

\- maximum contact force;

\- cumulative mouth/nose water exposure;

\- longest continuous exposure episode;

\- number of exposure episodes;

\- time to a durable mouth/nose-clear state.



Initial laboratory experiments are intended to use an aquatic rescue mannequin rather than human participants.



\## 4. Comparative Experimental Strategy



Candidate acquisition strategies may be compared under equivalent initial conditions.



These may include:



\- fixed predetermined acquisition;

\- conventional geometry-based acquisition;

\- airway-aware acquisition;

\- control conditions using delayed or otherwise non-causal airway information.



The purpose is to determine whether real-time airway-state feedback has a measurable causal effect on the acquisition process.



\## 5. Adaptive Hydrodynamic Action



A second research direction investigates whether intentionally controlled water flow can influence the state or orientation of a passive floating body without direct mechanical contact.



The initial physical question is:



\*\*Can a deliberately commanded external hydrodynamic field cause a passive, freely floating anthropomorphic body to transition from a respiratory-dangerous state toward a more favorable state without mechanical contact?\*\*



This capability is not assumed to exist.



The first objective is therefore to establish or reject the underlying physical effect experimentally.



\## 6. Progressive Hydrodynamic Validation



The proposed research progression is:



1\. test whether the physical effect exists;

2\. evaluate whether it can be controlled;

3\. test robustness across different initial conditions;

4\. investigate closed-loop control;

5\. only after physical validation, investigate whether AI-based control offers measurable advantages.



If the underlying physical effect is not demonstrated, the corresponding research branch stops.



\## 7. Role of Artificial Intelligence in Research



Artificial intelligence may later be investigated for closed-loop control under variable conditions including:



\- body position;

\- morphology;

\- buoyancy;

\- currents;

\- waves;

\- sensor uncertainty.



AI is not introduced merely to make an experimental mechanism appear intelligent.



Its use must be justified by measurable performance relative to simpler control strategies.



\## 8. Integration with Rescue Swarm



Experimental mechanisms are separated from the operational Rescue Swarm architecture until sufficient evidence supports integration.



This distinction allows the project to combine:



\- established engineering components;

\- prototype development;

\- experimental research;

\- future AI-controlled capabilities;



without presenting hypotheses as validated technology.



\## 9. Research Status



\*\*Ongoing research — 2026.\*\*



SAR-A and adaptive hydrodynamic action remain experimental research directions.



No positive experimental result is claimed in this repository unless supported by documented testing.
